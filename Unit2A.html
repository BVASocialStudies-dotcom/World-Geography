World Geography unit vocabulary

Climate Zones

A climate zone is a region with a similar long-term pattern of temperature and precipitation. Learn these ten terms, from the Arctic tundra to the tropical grasslands.

Polar

A very cold, dry climate found near the poles, where even the warmest month averages below 50°F (10°C).

Subarctic

A climate just south of the polar zone with long, bitterly cold winters and short, cool summers.

Humid Continental

A climate with four distinct seasons: cold, snowy winters and warm to hot, humid summers.

Humid Subtropical

A warm climate with hot, humid summers and mild, short winters, usually on the southeastern side of a continent.

Steppe

A semiarid climate that receives some rain, roughly 10 to 20 inches (25–50 cm) a year, but not enough to support forests.

Desert

An arid climate that receives less than about 10 inches (25 cm) of precipitation a year.

Undifferentiated Highland

A mountain climate that changes with elevation rather than latitude, so conditions can vary greatly over short distances.

Mediterranean Subtropical

A climate with hot, dry summers and mild, rainy winters, found along the western coasts of continents between about 30° and 40° latitude.

Marine West Coast

A mild, damp climate on the western coasts of continents, where winds off the ocean keep temperatures moderate all year.

Tropical Savanna

A hot climate near the tropics with a distinct wet season and dry season, also called tropical wet and dry.

Climate zones are based on the Köppen classification system, simplified for World Geography.
